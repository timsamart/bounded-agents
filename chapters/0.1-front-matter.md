## Reading paths

Pick a row. Stop when the stop condition is true. You do not need the rest of the document for that row to pay off.

| If you are… | Read… | Stop when… |
|---|---|---|
| An engineering or risk leader deciding whether to build | Part I (chapters 1–4) | You can defend the shape, or you have been told not to build it |
| An architect accountable for the shape | Parts I–II, then Appendix B | You can reconstruct every decision you are about to inherit |
| A platform engineer implementing one mechanism | **One chapter of Part II, opened cold** (see below), then Appendices D and E | You have bytes that validate |
| An SRE who inherited the system | Part III first | You can answer what fails, what fails closed, what can be stopped, and what happens to runs in flight |
| A security reviewer | Appendix C, then Appendix A, then chapter 21 | You know what remains possible after everything else is true |
| An auditor or supervisory reader | Appendix A only | The register answers threat → control → test → evidence |
| An architect who has already built one of these | Appendix B alone | You can inherit the reasoning, not only the shape |

### Cold-opening Part II

Part II is written so a single mechanism chapter can be opened without reading Parts I or the other Part II chapters. Each of chapters 5–11 starts with the plain claim in ordinary language, then the invariant codes. Part I vocabulary helps; it is not required. If a chapter needs a sibling, the orientation strip under its title says so in one line.

You can ignore every bracketed mark and the page still makes sense. That is how this was written. It is not an instruction about how to read.

## How to stay oriented

Every part divider and every main chapter opens with a short orientation strip: where you are, what you can decide or do after the chapter, and whether it is safe to open cold. Part dividers also list the child chapters and one-line functions. The strips are aids. Delete them and the argument still stands.

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Orientation (compass) | You-are-here strip |
| Part I (book) | Claim and shape |
| Part II (wrench) | Mechanisms; cold-openable by chapter |
| Part III (stop) | Operations |
| Part IV (edge) | Edges and residual |
| Exit (link) | Appendix or ADR pointer in the text |
| Path | A reading path in this front matter |

In the HTML and PDF builds the marks appear as small greyscale icons next to the strip labels. In plain markdown they are the words in the table.
